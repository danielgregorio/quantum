# PandaFoot — a football manager in the game language

> Status: approved 2026-10-22, the four phases below. Phase 1 done the same day; Phase 2 next.
> Laboratory tier: no tag here carries a stability promise until it ships.
> The game's design is [danielgregorio/pandafoot](https://github.com/danielgregorio/pandafoot)'s
> `GAME_DESIGN.md` (v0.2, with the decisions of 2026-10-22), and its numbers are checked there by
> `tools/simular_mundo.py`. This repository holds the game written in `.q`
> (`projects/pandafoot/`), what the language gains for it, and its tests.

## The decision

PandaFoot is a football manager after Elifoot 2: there is always a next match; you prepare it, play
it, see the result, and the season goes on. It is made in the game language (`qg:`, Godot), drawn
as an 80×25 text screen of the DOS days (a monospaced font, box borders, terminal colours): it looks
like the prompt Elifoot was and plays like a game.

Not in the UI Engine (`quantum console`), though its tables and forms would draw the management
screens well: the heart of PandaFoot is the **live round** — every match of the round in the same
minute, on every manager's screen, goals popping, the table moving, the horn and the chat — and that
is what the game runtime already is. Its lockstep with a seeded random source gives every machine the
same minute and the same goal; a time-out and the horn are inputs like a key; the chat is the
shared field; the sounds, the blinking goal and speeds 1x to 16x are there. And the play protocol
lets an agent play whole seasons, so how fun the numbers are can be measured, not guessed.

## Rules for every feature

Those of [PLAN_GAMES_3.md](PLAN_GAMES_3.md): the schema is the one place a tag is declared; the
reference and the editors' schemas are regenerated from it; a tag the compiler does not know is an
error with the line; every tag has a line in the intentions and a game in the dataset, every refusal
a line in the negative one; every feature has a test that runs in Godot (and a networked one when it
touches what the lockstep carries); `CHANGELOG.md` and `GAMES_PARITY.md` move with it. A feature
comes in when PandaFoot asks for it and it serves more than this game: a table of rows from the
state serves a shop, an inventory, a scoreboard; a screen of one's own in a networked game serves a
card game and a party game.

## The method

1. **The game first.** `projects/pandafoot/pandafoot.q` is written as it should read, before its
   tags exist; what the compiler refuses is the phase's list (as Bricks, Siege, Crates, Rally and
   Marches did).
2. **The tags,** each with its Godot test, until the game compiles and plays.
3. **The playtest.** An agent plays seasons through the play protocol and the numbers that say
   whether it is fun are measured (below); the person plays it with friends. What is not fun goes
   back to the design (GAME_DESIGN.md) or to the numbers (the simulator), and the game follows.

## Phase 1 — One league, alone or on one machine

The game: a league of 10 clubs (one division, home and away: 18 rounds), squads of 22 generated by
GAME_DESIGN v0.2 (força by the club's level, traits by band of força, a Gênio hidden in one of the
weakest clubs), for one manager or two to four on one machine (hot-seat).

- **Screens**, each a scene, a hotkey away: the dashboard (next match, balance, position, form), the
  squad (F3: a table of 22 players, força, positions, traits, morale, form), the line-up (F1: the
  formation's eleven and the bench), tactics (F2: formation, style, objective and the automatic
  reactions), the table (F7), finances (F5).
- **The live round**: the five matches together, the manager's in front and the other four on the
  round's scoreboard, the table moving with each goal; speeds 1x to 16x; a pause that is free alone
  and three time-outs each on one machine; the reactions changing tactics at their minute, with
  three seconds to cancel; goal, card and injury events from the design's match engine (strength
  by position, morale, form, traits, tactics and the counter-tactic matrix, home advantage, luck).
- **After the match**: the summary, gate and the week's wages, the updated table; the month's TV
  and sponsorship; the season's end with the champion.
- **Saves**: the whole world in a slot.

Expected to be asked for (confirmed when the game is written): a **table of rows from the state** (a
squad, a league table) in the HUD and in menus; **text formatting** (padding to a column, money as
`$1.500.000`, percentages); a **text-mode screen** (a monospaced font and box borders, maybe only a
font and a `qg:text` of several lines); reading and **writing a field of a record** (`team.points`),
in place, in an array of them; the world's size (220 players, 90 matches) in the state, the saves
and the replay's dump; a sound or two synthesized (goal, whistle) by `scripts/art/`.

**The playtest of Phase 1**, over 20 seasons played by an agent with a fixed plan (always the
objective the Auto mode picks): the champion is not the club of the highest average força in a
third of them or more; a weaker club (by 3 or more of average força) wins about a fifth to a
quarter of its matches; 2.3 to 3.2 goals a match; the counter-tactic matrix moves the win rate by
about the 10% it promises; the hidden Gênio scores more than any player of the clubs at its level; no club
of the top half goes broke in a season, and the bottom one feels the squeeze.

**Done 2026-10-22.** `projects/pandafoot/pandafoot.q`, in English with Portuguese and Spanish
(`qg:language`), tested by `tests/godot/test_godot_pandafoot.py` and the tags' own tests
(`test_godot_tables.py`, `TestManagementScreens`). What the game asked of the language:

- **A table of rows from the state** in the HUD: `qg:table source= as= rows=` and `qg:column label=
  value= width= align= color=` (the squad, the league table, the round's scoreboard, the news).
- **A menu of rows**: `source=` and `as=` on `qg:menu`, one `qg:button` shown for each row (the
  line-up, the clubs to choose); and menus of a player shown together share the keys as one column
  (the new game's form beside its list of clubs).
- **A screen laid out by hand**: `x=` and `y=` on `qg:hud` and `qg:menu`, `box=` on `qg:hud`; a
  colour read from the state (`color="{...}"` on `qg:text`). The text-mode screen needed nothing
  more: a monospaced font and a box drawn by `scripts/art/pandafoot.py`.
- **Text formatting**: `fit()`, `thousands()`, `repeat()`, and the Core's `join()`, `pick()` and
  `chance()`.
- **Records changed in place**: `q:set name="p.goals"`, and `index=` on a dictionary.
- **A world's number**: `seed="{world}"`; a scene's computed state set as it is entered, after the
  seed (the same world from the same number); the game's functions written after the scenes.
- The world's size in the state was no problem for the saves or the replays; it was for the play
  protocol, which wrote the whole state out twice a tick to find what changed, and now hashes it.

**The playtest.** An agent played 20 seasons of world 7 through the play protocol with the plan
written above (Lontra AC, a club of the middle, objective Auto, its eleven left as the computer
first picked it). The first run found what was not fun, and the bugs behind some of it:

| Measure | Target | First run | Now |
|---|---|---|---|
| Goals a match | 2.3 to 3.2 | 2.61 | 2.43 |
| The champion is not the strongest club (by average strength) | a third of the seasons or more | 20 of 20, but always one of two clubs | 16 of 20: each of the three big clubs, and twice a middle one, once another |
| A club weaker by 3 or more wins | a fifth to a quarter | 10% | 22% (and draws 26%) |
| The counter-tactics move the win rate | about 10% | 37% against 36% in even matches (they only touched the attack) | 40% against 32% in even matches (the model below) |
| The hidden genius outscores everyone else of the small clubs | yes | 0 goals: he played in goal | 16 of 20 seasons (6 to 14 goals; the best of the others 4 to 9) |
| No club of the top half goes broke; the bottom feels the squeeze | yes; yes | yes; every club made money | once in 100 top-half seasons a club went into the red; the small clubs end most seasons in it |

What went back to the numbers, through a model of the match engine in Python run on worlds dumped
from the game (it agrees with the game within a tenth of a goal), and to the design's decisions
(danielgregorio/pandafoot's `GAME_DESIGN.md`, decisions 10 to 13):

- **The automatic eleven** filled each place with the best at it, keepers first: half a genius
  midfielder beat a real keeper, so all three geniuses kept goal. Each place is now filled by those
  who play it, then by those for whom it is a second position, then by anyone.
- **A manager's tactics**: the objective's starting tactics come back at each kick-off (a match's
  late Kamikaze no longer carries into the next one), and a manager's own eleven is kept, unless
  someone in it cannot play.
- **Strength decides less**: a side's chance and finishing go by strength to the power of 0.7, luck
  is 0.8 to 1.2, a chance a minute 0.1.
- **Each round its own luck**: the round's scene had no seed, so it started from 0 every round and drew
  the same: one club took twenty titles in a row. It is seeded by the world, the season and the round.
- **The counter-tactics** lift or sink a side's defence as well as its attack; in even matches (within
  3 of strength) the side with the edge wins 40%, the side against it 32% (over all matches, where the
  stronger side tends to have the edge, 51% and 23%).
- **The economy of a league of three levels**: TV is $100.000 a month to every club and the costs
  $130.000 and $1 a seat; the hidden genius is paid as a small club pays, for his traits, since no
  one knows yet what he is (as a genius he cost a small club more than it earns).

Not in Phase 1, and felt: the same squads every season (no transfers, no ageing: Phase 3), so the
big clubs take most titles of a world; a club whose wages outrun its income sinks deeper every season
with nothing to stop it (the crisis and bankruptcy are Phase 3); a manager who never touches the eleven
tires it, and the agent's club finishes anywhere from 2nd to 10th.

## Phase 2 — The live round online

The design's default room: two to eight managers on their own machines (the lockstep, its seats,
watchers and relay rooms). Expected: **a screen of one's own** while the round is prepared (each
machine in its own scenes; only the decisions travel), **tactics kept secret until kick-off**, the
**time-outs** (three each, half a minute, stopping the round for all, everyone seeing who asked),
the **horn** (a sound on every machine, at most once a minute), the chat, the **human derby** (a
match of the round like the others), watchers.

Playtest: a round with the agent in two seats and a person in another; time-outs and the horn feel
right (not too many, not too few); the round's length with three time-outs each.

## Phase 3 — The rest of the club

Transfers (the salary auction with the acceptance draw, free agents and their signing fee, paying
in instalments), the stadium's health and its works, the financial crisis and bankruptcy, four
divisions with promotion and relegation, a cup, the calendar of both. Playtest: five seasons
from the fourth division; how often a lower club climbs; how often the hidden Gênio is found and
sold; whether the auction's long shots ("Romário topou o Náutico!") come often enough to be stories
and rarely enough to be news.

## Phase 4 — The asynchronous room

Only if the people who play the online room ask for it: a round played within a deadline, each
manager when they can, the human derby simulated once with each one's tactics and reactions. It needs
the round's state kept between sessions, which the relay does not keep today.
